# Biblical Masculinity: What the Church Won't Tell You

Your grandfather didn't need a podcast to tell him how to be a man.

He woke up. He worked. He provided. He protected. He led his family to church on Sunday and taught his sons with a firm hand and a straight back. He didn't read books about masculinity. He lived it.

Now look around. Churches run conferences on "authentic manhood." Pastors trip over themselves apologizing for anything that sounds too direct. And Christian men sit in small groups discussing their feelings while their families drift.

Biblical masculinity has become a topic of debate instead of a way of life.

That ends today.

## The Hard Truth About Biblical Masculinity

Biblical masculinity is not a cultural construct. It's not a reaction to feminism. It's not something the church invented to oppress women.

It's a command from God to every man who bears His image.

Here's what nobody's telling you: The soft Christianity you've been fed is killing you. The "gentle Jesus, meek and mild" version of faith has produced a generation of passive men who lead nothing, protect nothing, and stand for nothing.

The culture tells you masculinity is the problem. The church often agrees — just more politely. They'll let you lead the prayer breakfast but never say anything that might offend.

And meanwhile, your wife waits for you to step up. Your kids watch you defer. And you wonder why Sunday morning feels hollow.

Biblical masculinity isn't about dominance or aggression. It's about responsibility. It's about standing where God placed you and refusing to move.

## What Nobody's Telling You

I spent two decades hauling garbage for a living. Forty-pound bags. Six hundred houses a day. Before dawn until after dark.

You know what I learned on that truck?

Men don't need permission to lead. They need conviction.

Every morning I watched houses where the dad was absent — not physically, but spiritually. Nice cars in the driveway. Toys in the yard. But you could feel it. No man at the helm.

The Christian masculinity being taught in most churches is a neutered version of the real thing. They've sanded down the edges so nobody gets cut. They've removed anything that might make a man uncomfortable.

But Scripture isn't comfortable.

"Watch ye, stand fast in the faith, quit you like men, be strong." — 1 Corinthians 16:13

That word "quit" in the King James doesn't mean stop. The Greek is *andrizomai* — literally, "make yourself a man." Act like one. Conduct yourself with courage.

Paul didn't tell the Corinthians to explore their authentic selves. He told them to man up.

When's the last time you heard that from a pulpit?

## What God's Word Says

Scripture doesn't dance around godly masculinity. It commands it.

Look at Ephesians 5:25: "Husbands, love your wives, even as Christ also loved the church, and gave himself for it."

Gave himself. Sacrifice. Death.

That's the standard. Not doing the dishes sometimes. Not "helping out" with the kids. Dying.

Christ didn't love the church by asking what she needed and checking in about her feelings. He led. He served. He sacrificed everything — His comfort, His safety, His life.

Biblical masculinity isn't about bossing your wife around. It's about laying down your life for her every single day. It's about carrying weight she doesn't even know exists.

Consider Joshua 24:15: "But as for me and my house, we will serve the LORD."

Joshua didn't take a vote. He didn't ask his family's permission. He declared where he stood and where his house would stand. That's leadership.

Or look at 1 Timothy 5:8: "But if any provide not for his own, and specially for those of his own house, he hath denied the faith, and is worse than an infidel."

Worse than an infidel. That's not a suggestion. That's a warning.

You want to know what biblical masculinity looks like? It looks like a man who provides. Who protects. Who leads his family in prayer even when he doesn't feel like it. Who makes hard decisions and carries the weight of them.

## What This Means For Your Life

This isn't abstract theology. This is Monday morning.

It means you stop waiting for your wife to initiate spiritual conversations. You lead them.

It means you stop letting screens raise your children. You disciple them.

It means you stop drifting through work like it doesn't matter. You bring excellence because your labor is unto the Lord.

Biblical masculinity is lived out in the ordinary. In the daily. In the grind.

I used to get home from the garbage route bone-tired. Every muscle aching. Covered in whatever had leaked through those bags that day.

And my kids needed me. My wife needed me. Not the exhausted version. The present version.

That's the fight. Not some abstract battle against culture. The daily choice to show up when you'd rather check out.

### Practical Steps

**Lead family worship.** Tonight. Not next week. Get your family together, read a passage, pray. It doesn't have to be polished. It has to happen.

**Make a decision you've been avoiding.** You know the one. The conversation. The boundary. The choice. Stop deferring. Lead.

**Physical discipline.** Your body is a temple. Start treating it like one. A man who can't control his appetites will struggle to lead anything.

**Know Scripture.** Not just verses. The whole counsel of God. You can't lead your family spiritually if you're biblically illiterate.

## The Path Forward

The path to biblical masculinity isn't complicated. But it is hard.

It requires killing the passive version of yourself. The version that waits for permission. The version that keeps the peace at the cost of truth. The version that lets someone else carry the weight.

You weren't designed for comfort. You were designed for battle.

Christian masculinity in Scripture always involves conflict. David faced Goliath. Nehemiah rebuilt walls with a sword in one hand. Paul fought the good fight.

The soft gospel produces soft men. And soft men build nothing, protect nothing, pass on nothing.

The church needs men who will stand. Not perfectly — David wasn't perfect. Not without struggle — Paul had thorns. But men who refuse to sit down when God called them to stand.

### What's Keeping You Back

Let's name it.

Fear. Fear of what your wife will think if you suddenly start leading. Fear of failing in front of your kids. Fear of being mocked for taking faith seriously.

Or comfort. The couch is easier than the calling. Netflix requires less than discipleship. Drifting takes no effort.

Or pride. The belief that you've got time. That you'll step up later. That it'll happen eventually without intention.

Scripture speaks to all of it: "The fear of man bringeth a snare: but whoso putteth his trust in the LORD shall be safe." — Proverbs 29:25

Fear of man is a snare. It traps you in passivity. But trust in the Lord sets you free to lead without requiring approval.
