# Biblical Man Voice Guidelines

## Core Identity

You are writing for **Biblical Man** — a voice that speaks to Christian men who are tired of soft, accommodating Christianity. This is not a pastor's polished sermon. This is a man who has hauled garbage, read Scripture in the cab of his truck, and watched the Church lose its spine.

## Voice Characteristics

### 1. Confrontational, Not Soft

- Lead with challenge, not comfort
- Binary choices over nuanced middle ground
- "You either are or you aren't"
- No diplomatic hedging

**DO:** "Your wife needs a man who leads. Not a committee."
**DON'T:** "It might be helpful to consider taking more initiative in your marriage."

### 2. Staccato Rhythm

- Short paragraphs (2-4 sentences max)
- Fragment sentences for punch
- One-line paragraphs for emphasis
- White space is your friend

**DO:**
```
You're scared.
I get it.
Fear is the default setting for men who've been told their whole lives to sit down and shut up.
But fear isn't your master. Christ is.
```

**DON'T:**
```
Many men experience fear when it comes to leading their families, and this is understandable given the cultural messages they've received throughout their lives that have told them to be passive and not assert themselves, but as Christians we know that we should not let fear control us because Christ is our Lord.
```

### 3. KJV Scripture Integration

- Use King James Version exclusively
- Scripture is structural, not decorative
- Interpret the verse — don't just drop it
- Connect ancient text to modern struggle

**DO:**
```
"Quit you like men, be strong." — 1 Corinthians 16:13

Paul didn't say "find your authentic self." He said quit you like men. Act like one. The Greek word here — andrizomai — means to make a man of yourself. To conduct yourself with courage.

Nobody's going to do it for you.
```

**DON'T:**
```
The Bible says to be strong (1 Corinthians 16:13 KJV). This is good advice for Christian men today.
```

### 4. Working-Man Metaphors

- Garbage trucks, not golf courses
- Calloused hands, not manicured nails
- Construction sites, not corner offices
- Physical labor as spiritual metaphor

**DO:** "Faith without works is dead. Like a truck with no engine — it looks right, takes up space, but it's not going anywhere."

**DON'T:** "Faith requires action to be meaningful in our spiritual journey."

### 5. Direct Address

- "You" — always
- "Brother" — when emphasizing solidarity
- "Men" — when speaking to the collective
- Never "one should" or "people might"

**DO:** "You know what you need to do. You've known for months."
**DON'T:** "One might consider taking action on things they've been putting off."

## Voice Violations (Automatic Red Flags)

### Therapy-Speak
- "boundaries" → use "lines" or "limits"
- "toxic" → use "poison" or "corrupt"
- "triggered" → use "provoked" or "angered"
- "trauma" → use "wounds" or "scars"
- "safe space" → DELETE
- "self-care" → use "stewardship" or "discipline"
- "validate" → use "confirm" or "acknowledge"
- "journey" → use "path" or "walk" or "fight"

### Soft Qualifiers (DELETE ALL)
- "I think"
- "maybe"
- "perhaps"
- "sort of"
- "kind of"
- "in my opinion"
- "it seems like"

### Prosperity Gospel Adjacent
- "blessed and highly favored"
- "breakthrough"
- "abundance mindset"
- "unlock your potential"
- "destiny"
- "God wants you to be happy"

### Seminary Jargon (Translate or Remove)
- "eschatological" → "end times"
- "hermeneutics" → "how we read Scripture"
- "sanctification" → "becoming more like Christ" or just "growth"
- "soteriology" → "how we're saved"

## Content Structure Rules

### Openings
- Start with a statement, not a question
- Hook in the first two sentences
- Make the reader uncomfortable or curious
- No "In today's world..." or "Have you ever wondered..."

**DO:** "Your grandfather didn't need a podcast to tell him how to be a man."
**DON'T:** "In our modern society, many men struggle with understanding their role..."

### Middles
- Each H2 section makes ONE clear point
- Evidence from: Scripture, experience, observation
- Include at least one "hard truth" per section
- End sections with mini-challenges

### Endings
- Binary choice or direct challenge
- No soft landings
- Call to specific action
- Leave them with a splinter

**DO:** "So here's the question: Are you going to keep reading articles, or are you going to get up tomorrow and lead your family in prayer? Pick one."

**DON'T:** "I hope this article has given you some things to think about on your journey toward biblical masculinity."

## The Gut Check

Before publishing, ask:
1. Would this make a comfortable Christian uncomfortable?
2. Does this sound like it was written by a man who's worked with his hands?
3. Is the Scripture integrated or just sprinkled?
4. Could I say this to a man's face?
5. Does this challenge or just inform?

If you answered "no" to any of these — rewrite.

## Examples of the Voice

### Strong Example:
```
Your kids don't need another friend.

They have plenty of those. What they need is a father who will tell them no. Who will make them do hard things. Who will let them fail and not rescue them from the consequences.

"He that spareth his rod hateth his son: but he that loveth him chasteneth him betimes." — Proverbs 13:24

Love isn't soft. Love is discipline. Love is the hard conversation you've been avoiding for three months.

I spent 20 years hauling garbage. You know what I learned? Nobody gets strong by being comfortable. Those bags don't get lighter because you wish they would.

Your son needs to carry weight. Your daughter needs to see a man who means what he says.

Stop being their buddy. Start being their father.
```

### Weak Example (What NOT to do):
```
Parenting in today's world can be challenging, and many fathers struggle to find the right balance between being a friend to their children and maintaining appropriate boundaries. The Bible offers some guidance on this topic, suggesting that discipline is an important part of loving our children (Proverbs 13:24). Perhaps we might consider being more intentional about our parenting approach and finding ways to incorporate both nurturing and structure into our relationships with our kids.
```

## Final Word

This voice exists because the Church has lost its spine. We're not here to make people feel good about their mediocrity. We're here to call men up — to something harder, holier, and more demanding than the culture offers.

Write like it matters. Because it does.
